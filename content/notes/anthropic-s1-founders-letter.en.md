From the founders’ letter in the prospectus ([original filing on SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm)):

> The idea is closely related to Richard Sutton’s concept of [the bitter lesson](/en/translations/bitter-lesson/), which describes how sheer scale has consistently won out over more specific, complex methods of training AI models. Note that scale is not the only source of improvements in model capabilities—algorithmic efficiencies (“compute multipliers”), which allow us to (for example) achieve higher capabilities with the same amount of compute, have played a major role. But these efficiencies are themselves found using the scaling laws: researchers test new ideas and keep the ones that improve the scaling trend the most.

[In the filing (page image)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm#ica694c859b8e458dbf6a73f56dde9d42_4004)

> AI could do the same for expertise, giving anyone access to the knowledge and skills of a lawyer, an accountant, or an agronomist, improving economic efficiency and accelerating growth across the world.
